# SaludConTech Slack Community Digest

**Source:** Slack export `SaludConTech _ Latino Health Innovation Alliance Slack export Oct 14 2017 - Mar 1 2026` (66 channel directories, ~1,195 JSON files, ~13MB)
**Analysis date:** 2026-08-11
**Privacy:** All member names and direct identifiers removed. Quotes paraphrased; no raw message text reproduced at length.
**Note:** The export folder label says Oct 2017 but the actual message data begins **Nov 2022** (workspace launch) and the last activity is **June 2025**. No member/analytics CSVs were found in the export directory despite the task description; only `users.json` (659 users, 220 deleted, 342 active non-bot) was available.

## 1. Channel Inventory

**Scale:** 65 channels, **4,619 messages total**, 196 unique human posters, 3,896 non-system text messages.

## 2. Recurring Themes, Member Asks, Pain Points, Wins

### What members asked for (recurring)
1. **Connections & intros** — "connect me with X", intros to founders, mentors, researchers, fellow premeds, fellow clinicians. Most common single ask.
2. **Jobs & opportunities** — jobs shared heavily; members reply "I'm applying!"; specific asks for AI/documentation roles, Salesforce experience, clinical collaboration.
3. **Funding & grants** — scholarships, fellowships (digital health equity fellowship, NIA accelerator), NIH/CDC/HRSA/HCAI grants; one member proposed a "report card" tracking which grants reach Latino-serving CBOs.
4. **Spanish-language tools & learning** — someone asked for a Spanish crash course to reach their community; repeated interest in Spanish AI tools and culturally adapted CGMs/diabetes apps.
5. **Co-working space / office space** — DTLA co-working recommendations, rooftop Sunday co-working ritual.
6. **Volunteer/leadership roles** — repeated "how do I get involved" — answered by Leadership Team + Student Ambassador program recruitment.
7. **Expertise recruitment** — malaria diagnostics collab, op-ed co-authors, video editing, translation/consent forms.

### Pain points (what frustrated members)
- **Channel sprawl** — leadership explicitly removed several channels after "too many channels was overwhelming" feedback; new-channel workflow was being made democratic.
- **Onboarding friction** — volunteers not receiving calendar invites; email address bounces; "how do I join the meeting?" repeated multiple times.
- **Tool/password chaos** — Bitwarden password gaps for Instagram/Facebook Business logins; volunteers locked out of org accounts; multiple scheduling tools (Zoom/Mixmax) with no clear owner.
- **Paywalled news** — members frequently shared paywalled articles (Tribune, USA Today) and asked for copies; 12ft.io workaround shared.
- **Geographic isolation** — non-LA members (KC, Chicago, Miami, SD) repeatedly asked for local activity; city channels sat empty.
- **Small-cell reach limits** — campaign work (Medicaid) hit capacity: members wanted an "elevator speech" approved to take to local clinics; radio/media pickups were celebrated as wins.

### Wins (what the community celebrated)
- **Campaign wins:** Medicaid unwinding ambassador program with state-by-state volunteers; member heard on Chicago radio; op-eds drafted; ICE detention health coverage pushed in media; 77 detained people vaccine effort.
- **Conference presence:** "Latino @ ViVE" meetup, HIMSS booth, NHMA dinner hosted by a member founder; conference talks by members; post-conference "great meeting you all!" waves.
- **Recognition:** Leader of the Month program, volunteer hour milestones (trophy emoji), HIMSS Changemaker finalist votes.
- **Community content:** conference videos (Digital Health Equity Conference), YouTube channel, "What Future Physicians Hope for in Digital Health" panel, Flamin' Hot screening with Q&A.
- **Personal wins:** medical school acceptances/applications supported by the group; career pivots; founders launching tools (Spanish dementia detection app fundraiser, CGMS tool for Spanish-speaking diabetics).

### What people valued most (evidence-ranked)
1. **The "familia" reception** — every intro got a warm bilingual welcome, often with a personalized connection ("I also do clinical trials research!", "we have SCT members in LA — let's organize a meetup").
2. **Being seen/recognized** — Leader of the Month, volunteer milestones, "we need more Latinas like you in healthcare."
3. **In-person meetups at conferences** — the conf-* channels show the most spontaneous joy and coordination.
4. **Fast intros to useful people** — leadership personally connected new members to relevant peers within hours.
5. **Spanish + cultural fluency** — "Hola familia", "cuídense mucho", "la plazita", identity affirmation (Mexican, Puerto Rican, Chapina, Dreamer — all stated proudly).

## 3. Voice & Tone Signals

- **Language:** ~97% English with deliberate Spanish seasoning ("Hola familia", "Bienvenidos", "¡Mil gracias!", "cuídense mucho", "Hasta pronto", "Aqui estoy", "Adonde están"). Code-switching is identity-affirming, not functional.
- **Framing vocabulary:** "familia", "la plazita", "digital barrio", "pachanga", "movers and shakers", "digital health equity", "our community needs us". The community's own words: **familia, community, equity, innovation, service**.
- **Warmth ratio:** exceptionally high — welcomes, thank-yous, emoji reactions, "congrats!", "proud of you" dominate. Confrontation is nearly absent.
- **Motivation framing:** *duty to community* ("I have a duty to help others who came before and after me"), *representation* ("we need more of us in these rooms"), *mission urgency* ("Let's not wait for the next pandemic to teach people of color telemedicine").
- **Identity:** members state heritage proudly (Mexican, Mexican-Puerto Rican, Colombian, Chapina, Dreamer, 1st-gen) and cite it as motivation.
- **Humor/banter:** light and self-deprecating (Forrest Gump marathoner, "pre-md/phd egomaniac", Dracula joke in an expert-seeking post).
- **Tone of leadership:** plainspoken, encouraging, operational — "who wants to join me?", "I'll keep you in the loop", direct asks with clear next steps.
- **Contrast to avoid on the website:** the community is *warm and familial*, NOT corporate/clinical. Copy that reads "professional network" undersells them; copy that reads "hermandad" resonates.

## 4. Signals for Membership Tiers, Events, and Sponsorship Pages

### Membership tiers
- **Volunteer/Ambassador tier already exists and is loved** — Student Ambassador program, Leadership Team recruitment, volunteer hours + recognition milestones. Strong evidence members *want* levels of involvement ("Sign me up", "I would like to do that here in KS/MO").
- **What members would pay/commit for (from behavior):**
  - **Intros & warm connections** — the #1 valued service.
  - **Funding/grant intelligence** — members actively share opportunities; a curated "Latino-serving grant tracker" was explicitly requested.
  - **Mentorship pairing** — premeds/residents/fellows repeatedly ask; mentorship was proposed in meeting notes (one-email mentorship model admired).
  - **Job board** — already exists (techqueria integration); a paid tier could add curated health-tech roles for Latino professionals.
  - **Spanish-language AI/tech skill-building** — recurring interest.
- **Donor-tier signals:** "fundraiser for Spanish dementia detection app" shared by a member founder; grant applications were org-level, but individual members regularly signaled willingness to fundraise/give (fabric mask donations, grants pass donations).
- **Pain point a tier could fix:** "too many channels" — a paid tier could be positioned as *access to the curated inner circle* (intros, office hours, funding watch) rather than more surface area.

### Events
- **Conferences are the strongest moments** — ViVE, HIMSS, NHMA drove real coordination and new-member conversions ("come ask a question", booth invites, dinner hosting). The conf-* channels are the best source of event-page language: spontaneous, live, "where are you?" energy.
- **Smaller recurring formats that worked:** monthly volunteer meetings, Sunday DTLA rooftop co-working (pomodoro + farmers market brunch), Friendsgiving/Cena, city pickleball/beach days, LA Tech Week group outings.
- **The "Health Equity Fiesta"** (flipped health fairs, CBOs offering services) is a differentiated format worth featuring — members engaged around it and it maps to real community need.
- **Event-page language to steal (paraphrased):** "Come share your wins, needs, and how we can support and uplift each other. The holidays can be tough, so just come and say hi and make new friends." This is the house voice in the wild.

### Sponsorship
- **Community calendar with 100+ events over two weeks** (Tech Week) was the flagship sponsorship-era asset; events were free via RSVP.
- **Partners who hosted/attended:** Techstars (Google HQ), ScaleHealth (taco event), Techqueria (co-branded meetup), LA Tech4Good (20% code), Casa, ASI (NHMA dinner), a member's digital health consultancy, People Science (sleep study), Angeles Investors (Top 100 Hispanic-led startups).
- **Sponsor-proof moments to feature:** HIMSS booth, "Latino @ ViVE" meetup, NHMA dinner hosted by a member founder, conference video series, Flamin' Hot screening Q&A.
- **What sponsors would buy (evidence):** audience = physicians, residents, premeds, public health pros, founders, engineers — the exact buyer personas health-tech sponsors want; plus an "ecosystem contact list" that members actively built (one member found 100+).
- **Caveat for sponsor pages:** members are equity-first and mission-driven; sponsorship copy should emphasize *supporting Latino leadership and community health*, not "diverse talent pipeline" phrasing (tone in the export suggests "health equity"/"familia" language, not corporate DEI jargon).

## 5. Risks & Surprises

1. **No CSV files exist** despite the task description mentioning member/analytics CSVs. Only `users.json` (659 users; 342 active, 220 deleted) is available. Role-title data is sparse (309 of 342 active users have no title), so role demographics are approximate.
2. **The community is largely dormant in Slack** — the real activity window was Nov 2022 – Jun 2023, with a ViVE 2024 bump. The website should not imply an active Slack community without a revival plan. Treat the export as *historical voice evidence*, not a live community snapshot.
3. **The founder's name, phone number, and home address appear in the raw export** (posted publicly in channels, e.g., Superbowl open invite). If the export is ever shared or archived publicly, this is a personal-privacy liability.
4. **Personal PII is pervasive** (phone numbers, home listings, family details, immigration status "Dreamer round 1", employer names). The digest is safe, but any further use of the raw export must stay private.
5. **Auto-generated bot content is significant** (bot digests, newsletter mirrors, RoleUp test messages) — ~10-15% of raw messages. Any future quantitative analysis should filter bots before counting.
6. **Name collisions with the Ligazon voice rules:** the export contains heavy use of "health equity" and em-dash-adjacent punctuation in member copy. The digest preserves meaning but does not reproduce raw copy, so no conflict with the house voice rules in this file.

## Appendix: Method

- Parsed all 65 channel directories (1,195 JSON files) in the export.
- De-identified every message (user mentions → `@m`, names stripped); role labels derived from profile titles (keyword-bucketed) for context only.
- Computed per-channel counts, per-month volume, unique posters, and Spanish-language share (3.2% strongly Spanish; code-switching far more common than full Spanish).
- Read full text of 25+ channels covering every category: core, city, region, role, interest, project, conference, ops.
- No raw message text reproduced at length anywhere in this digest.
